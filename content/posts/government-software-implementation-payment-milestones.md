# Getting Paid During Implementation: Milestones and Holdbacks on Government Software Contracts

**You can staff a government implementation for months, hit every deadline, and still not see the last of your money until long after go-live.**

*By Domenic Giovanzana, Founder and General Partner, Lambda Capital. Last updated October 13, 2026.*

In the government software contracts we reviewed, implementation is paid by milestone: you invoice only after the agency accepts a defined deliverable, and the agency keeps a holdback from each payment until final acceptance or the end of warranty. The holdbacks in those documents ranged from 5 to 20 percent. That range describes these specific contracts, not the market. Federal commercial contracts can include interim payments and advance payments capped at 15 percent of contract price. You can often propose your own schedule, so negotiate it before you sign.

*This post is general information, not legal or accounting advice. Read your own contract and talk to counsel before you rely on any of it.*

## Why implementation is where the cash gap is widest

The subscription is the easy part. In an earlier post on [upfront subscription billing](https://www.lambdavp.com/blog/government-saas-upfront-payment) I covered how a fixed term license can be billed in advance. Implementation is different. You staff the project for months, but payment waits on acceptance, then on the invoice cycle, then on a holdback release.

Acceptance usually gates the invoice. The Tampa Hillsborough Expressway Authority's payment exhibit says the contractor may invoice for a payment milestone only "once the Contractor completes and has received the Authority's approval of every deliverable associated with a payment milestone." ([Tampa Hillsborough Expressway Authority, Exhibit A](https://tampa-xway.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/T-2325-OBOS-BP-Exhibit-A-Project-Payment-Milestones-Addendum3-3.26.25.pdf))

Then the payment clock starts. Connecticut's agreement says the Department pays "within forty-five (45) days after Deliverables have been accepted by the Department and an invoice in accordance with the Project Implementation Schedule has been received." ([Connecticut agreement](https://www.biznet.ct.gov/SCP_Documents/Bids/24905/Attachment%204-Information%20Processing%20Systems%20Agreement.pdf)) A 2008 City of Los Angeles contract says the City "will make payment within forty-five (45) calendar days after receipt of written invoice from Contractor." ([City of Los Angeles contract](https://cityclerk.lacity.org/onlinedocs/2007/07-2186-S1_misc_7-7-08.pdf)) If those clocks slip, see [prompt payment laws for GovTech vendors](https://www.lambdavp.com/blog/prompt-payment-laws-govtech-software-vendors).

## How state and local agencies structure implementation payments

Three building blocks show up again and again: milestone payments tied to accepted deliverables, a holdback withheld from each milestone, and a release trigger such as system acceptance, the end of warranty, or post go-live defect resolution.

**Examples from state and local contracts** (each percentage belongs to the named document only):

| Buyer and document | How implementation is paid | Holdback | Holdback release | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Connecticut, Information Processing Systems Agreement | Within 45 days after deliverable accepted and invoiced | 10% of each accepted deliverable | Half after system acceptance period, half after warranty | [CT agreement](https://www.biznet.ct.gov/SCP_Documents/Bids/24905/Attachment%204-Information%20Processing%20Systems%20Agreement.pdf) |
| Colorado Dept. of State, SCORE II (2006 contract) | Per Payment and Delivery Schedule | 20% per deliverable (except hardware and third party software) | 50% at each milestone, balance after 60 day onsite support period | [CO contract](https://files.causeofamerica.org/docs/2006-sep-14-co-dept-of-state-score-ii-saber-software-contract-with-corrections-oct-2006-rev.pdf) |
| CCCERA (Contra Costa County Employees' Retirement Association), Schedule D | At milestones specified in the schedule | 15% per milestone | 7.5% of total project cost at Final System Acceptance, 7.5% after 6 month warranty | [CCCERA Schedule D](https://www.cccera.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/cccera_-_schedule_d_charges_final.pdf) |
| New York ITS, IES Tier II Lot 3 contract | On completion and acceptance of deliverables or milestones | 15% of each invoice | In stages, final 10% tied to defect resolution up to 18 months after go-live | [NY ITS contract](https://its.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/06/c000657_ies_tier-ii_lot-3_deloitte_contract.pdf) |
| Tampa Hillsborough Expressway Authority, Exhibit A | Invoice only after every deliverable in the milestone is approved; 5% at mobilization | 5% of each milestone | At System Acceptance | [Tampa Exhibit A](https://tampa-xway.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/T-2325-OBOS-BP-Exhibit-A-Project-Payment-Milestones-Addendum3-3.26.25.pdf) |
| Mississippi Division of Medicaid, RFP cost form (proposed terms) | Deliverable schedule; vendor may propose an alternative | 15% on pre-implementation deliverables | After warranty period | [MS cost form](https://medicaid.ms.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/REVISED-2-COST-INFORMATION-SUBMISSION-004.pdf) |

New York is the long tail example. Its contract says "The State will withhold fifteen percent (15%) of each invoice submitted upon acceptance of a Deliverable/Milestone," and the last tranche is a "Release of ten percent (10%) of the withheld payment for resolution of all new critical defects within a timeframe established in the Tier II Assignment, but up to within eighteen (18) months, of Go-Live." ([NY ITS contract](https://its.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/06/c000657_ies_tier-ii_lot-3_deloitte_contract.pdf)) Part of what you earned on that kind of contract can sit with the agency for a year and a half after launch.

## What is negotiable: a vendor playbook

1. **Propose your own schedule.** Mississippi's Medicaid cost form says "Vendor may propose an alternative payment schedule to be finalized during contract negotiations." ([MS cost form](https://medicaid.ms.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/REVISED-2-COST-INFORMATION-SUBMISSION-004.pdf))
2. **Ask for an early mobilization milestone.** The Tampa exhibit lists "Mobilization (30 days after NTP) | 5%". ([Tampa Exhibit A](https://tampa-xway.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/T-2325-OBOS-BP-Exhibit-A-Project-Payment-Milestones-Addendum3-3.26.25.pdf))
3. **Weight payments to effort.** Mississippi says amounts "should correlate to the level of effort and criticality of the deliverable/milestone to the project." Front-loaded work deserves front-loaded payment.
4. **Counter a high holdback.** In a Jefferson Parish, Louisiana procurement, the proposed terms called for "a twenty percent (20%) holdback of each invoice for the implementation service costs." One vendor responded by suggesting "a retainage amount of 5%" of each implementation services invoice, "to be released upon the successful completion of each phase of the implementation." ([Jefferson Parish agenda attachment](https://jp-appserver.jeffparish.net/AGENDA/12062023/related1262023/61kn01!.PDF)) That was one vendor's proposal. The document does not show the final term.
5. **Release the holdback per phase, not only at the end.** Colorado's 2006 contract released half of each milestone's holdback at the milestone, and the Jefferson Parish vendor asked for release at each phase.
6. **Define acceptance periods in writing.** Pennsylvania's IT terms say "Unless otherwise agreed to by the Commonwealth in the Acceptance Plan, the acceptance period shall be 10 business days for interim items and 30 business days for final items." ([PA IT terms](https://www.emarketplace.state.pa.us/FileDownload.aspx?file=SJF%5C%5C49034%5C%5C49034_Terms+and+Conditions+IT.pdf)) A clear clock keeps silence from stalling payment.
7. **Keep subscription and support on separate lines.** Price the license and support on their own lines so only services carry the holdback, and bill the subscription upfront where the rules allow (see [upfront subscription billing](https://www.lambdavp.com/blog/government-saas-upfront-payment)).

## Federal contracts: commercial interim and advance payments, and performance-based payments

**Federal financing rules** (these are financing terms, not holdbacks):

| Rule | What it allows | Limit or condition | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial purchases (FAR 32.202) | Commercial interim and advance payments when in the government's best interest | Advance payments may not exceed 15% of contract price | [FAR 32.202-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.202-1), [32.202-2](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.202-2) |
| Noncommercial (FAR 32.10) | Performance-based payments, preferred when practical and the contractor agrees | Not for cost-reimbursement line items or sealed bid awards; fully recoverable on default | [FAR 32.1001](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.1001) |

On commercial purchases, FAR starts from the vendor's side: "financing of the contract is normally the contractor's responsibility." But "the contracting officer may include appropriate financing terms in contracts for commercial purchases when doing so will be in the best interest of the Government." Conditions include "The contract price exceeds the simplified acquisition threshold" and "Adequate security is obtained." ([FAR 32.202-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.202-1))

Commercial advance payments are payments "made before any performance of work under the contract. The aggregate of these payments shall not exceed 15 percent of the contract price." They are "not subject to the interest penalty provisions of the Prompt Payment Act." ([FAR 32.202-2](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.202-2)) That is a different tool from upfront subscription billing.

For noncommercial work, "Performance-based payments are the preferred Government financing method when the contracting officer finds them practical, and the contractor agrees to their use." They are not used for payments under cost-reimbursement line items or contracts awarded through sealed bids, and they "are fully recoverable, in the same manner as progress payments, in the event of default." ([FAR 32.1001](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/32.1001))

The practical point: none of this is automatic. These are contract terms you ask for during negotiation.

## When the schedule still leaves a gap

Even a well negotiated schedule leaves months of delivery cost ahead of cash. So most GovTech companies with long receivables end up with a financing tool alongside their contract terms: an early payment program if their agency offers one, factoring on large one-time invoices, a credit line, or capital backed by the signed contract. ## Frequently asked questions

### How are government software implementations usually paid?

In the contracts we reviewed, by milestone. The vendor invoices after the agency accepts a defined deliverable or phase, and the agency withholds a holdback from each payment. Connecticut, New York, CCCERA, and Tampa's expressway authority all tie payment to acceptance.

### What is a holdback on a government IT contract?

A percentage of each milestone payment the agency keeps until a later event, such as final system acceptance or the end of warranty. Published examples include 10 percent in Connecticut, 15 percent at CCCERA and New York, and 20 percent in a 2006 Colorado contract. These are examples, not a standard.

### When is the holdback released?

It depends on the contract. Connecticut releases half after the acceptance period and half after warranty. CCCERA pays 7.5 percent of total project cost at final acceptance and 7.5 percent six months later. New York releases the withheld amount in stages up to 18 months after go-live.

### Can I propose my own payment schedule in an RFP?

Often yes. Mississippi's Medicaid cost form says the vendor may propose an alternative payment schedule to be finalized in negotiations, and asks that payment amounts match the effort and criticality of each milestone. Read the RFP's cost and payment sections for similar language.

### Can I get paid at the start of a government software project?

Sometimes. Tampa's expressway authority lists a 5 percent mobilization payment, 30 days after notice to proceed. On federal commercial contracts, advance payments are allowed under conditions, but their total cannot exceed 15 percent of the contract price before work starts.

### Can I negotiate a lower holdback?

You can ask. In Jefferson Parish, Louisiana, a software vendor responded to a proposed 20 percent holdback by suggesting 5 percent of each implementation invoice, released at the completion of each phase. The document does not show the final term. If the gap remains, see our guide on government contract financing for software companies.

### Do federal agencies offer milestone payments?

Yes, through contract financing terms. For noncommercial work, FAR calls performance-based payments the preferred financing method when practical and the contractor agrees. For commercial products and services, contracting officers may include commercial interim and advance payments when in the government's best interest.

### What if the agency never formally accepts a deliverable?

Negotiate acceptance periods in writing. Pennsylvania's IT terms set 10 business days for interim items and 30 business days for final items unless agreed otherwise. Once a deliverable is accepted and invoiced, know your rights under prompt payment laws for GovTech vendors if payment is late.
