# Can Government Customers Pay Your SaaS Subscription Upfront? A GovTech Vendor's Guide

**Most GovTech vendors bill in arrears because they assume they have to. Often they don't.**

*By Domenic Giovanzana, Founder and General Partner, Lambda Capital. Last updated October 12, 2026.*

Often yes for federal buyers, with conditions. GSA guidance says upfront payment for a fixed price, fixed term SaaS license is not a prohibited advance payment because access starts when payment is made. Professional services and usage based cloud still get paid in arrears. State and local rules vary: some statutes expressly allow prepaying software subscriptions, while some state contracts prohibit prepayment. Check your contract and jurisdiction.

*This post is general information, not legal or accounting advice. Confirm the rules for your contract with your contracting officer and counsel.*

## Why billing in arrears hurts GovTech cash flow

When you bill in arrears, you deliver first and invoice later. Your receivable cycle then sits on top of the delivery period, so a renewal you already won still costs you months of cash before it pays. Many government software vendors bill this way because they assume the buyer requires it. Sometimes it does. Often it is worth asking.

The federal starting point is strict. Under 31 U.S.C. 3324, "a payment under a contract to provide a service or deliver an article for the United States Government may not be more than the value of the service already provided or the article already delivered." An advance "may be made only if it is authorized by" a "specific appropriation or other law." ([31 U.S.C. 3324](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A31+section%3A3324+edition%3Aprelim%29))

## The federal rule: advance payments are restricted, but upfront SaaS is different

GSA puts it plainly: "Advance payments to Government contractors for goods and services are generally prohibited unless specifically authorized." Its Acquisition Letter MV-2024-01, issued March 15, 2024, then "clarifies that under specific conditions, addressed below, an upfront payment is not considered an advance payment." The reasoning: "The central distinction is the contemporaneous access." When your customer gets the software at the moment they pay, they have received what they paid for. ([GSA MV-2024-01](https://www.gsa.gov/policy-regulations/policy/acquisition-policy/acquisition-policy-library-and-resources/mv202401))

The letter "is effective immediately and remains in effect until rescinded or incorporated into the GSAM." Note one thing other write-ups get wrong: GSA does not say it "approves advance payment." It says a qualifying upfront SaaS payment is not an advance payment at all. Use GSA's wording when you talk to a contracting officer.

## The six GSA criteria: a checklist for your quote

Per [GSA MV-2024-01](https://www.gsa.gov/system/files/MV-2024-01.pdf), upfront payment qualifies when:

1. "Access to the software is granted contemporaneously with payment."
2. "The license is acquired on a fixed-price or fixed-price with economic price adjustment basis."
3. "The license is priced at a single seat, multi-seat, unit, or subscription price covering a fixed term."
4. "The license's pricing/billing model allows for no utilization or consumption metric other than quantity to affect the costs incurred over the negotiated term."
5. "The license does not require any upfront payment other than the fixed seat, unit, or subscription cost as a prerequisite for access or a pricing discount."
6. "The license service is continuous and uninterrupted for the negotiated term of access to the license."

Keep the license separate from everything else. GSA's MAS solicitation says "All other cloud computing services (i.e., IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and professional services must be paid in arrears in accordance with 31 U.S.C. § 3324 and 41 U.S.C. § 4505." ([GSA MAS solicitation](https://sws.gsa.gov/sws-search/viewSolDocument.do?method=view&solDoc=MDkwMDI5YjQ4Y2I5OTc4MQ%3D%3D&solNum=NDdRU01EMjBSMDAwMQ%3D%3D&solRefresh=MjY%3D)) Implementation, training, and usage based charges belong on their own lines.

## What GSA's ordering guidance adds

GSA's Cloud SIN ordering guidance says "Upfront annual SaaS payments are not prohibited 'advance payments' because the SaaS is 'delivered' upon provisioning." It also warns that multi-year deals must respect the Bona Fide Needs rule: "Agencies cannot obligate funds for future years without multi-year contracting authority." ([GSA Cloud SIN ordering guidance](https://www.gsa.gov/technology/it-contract-vehicles-and-purchasing-programs/multiple-award-schedule-it/cloud-and-cloud-related-services/cloud-sin-ordering-guidance))

The practical takeaway: ask for annual upfront billing for each option year, not a multi-year prepayment.

One scope caveat. This is GSA guidance for GSA contracting activities and MAS cloud orders. It does not automatically bind every federal agency contract. Confirm with your contracting officer.

## Federal, state and local rules compared

The federal rules are fairly uniform, but state and local rules depend on the statute and the contract vehicle, so here is how they compare side by side.

**Minnesota** is the clearest example of a state that writes prepayment into law. Counties may prepay software development or maintenance services for county owned or leased data processing equipment ([Minn. Stat. 375.84](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/375.84)), and the commissioner may allow state agencies to prepay software, software maintenance, and "information technology hosting services" ([Minn. Stat. 16A.065](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/16A.065)).

**Florida** lets the clerk of the circuit court make advance payments for the county, "including, but not limited to, maintenance agreements and subscriptions, pursuant to rules or procedures adopted by the Chief Financial Officer." ([Fla. Stat. 28.235](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/28.235))

**Massachusetts** goes the other way on its statewide IT contracts: "No prepayment should be made for products not yet delivered or services not yet rendered." ([ITS64](https://www.statewidecontractuserguide.mass.gov/CUG/Guide/ITS64/E?Category=ITS)) Whether a SaaS term that starts on payment counts as "delivered" is a question for the buyer and counsel. I am raising it as a discussion point, not answering it.

Before you ask, check three things: your state's statute, the cooperative or statewide contract you sell through, and the agency's own finance policy.

## How to ask for upfront annual billing

1. **Price the subscription as a fixed term, fixed price line,** mirroring the GSA criteria.
2. **Start access on the payment or term start date,** and state continuous access in your license terms.
3. **Keep services and usage on separate lines** billed in arrears.
4. **On federal deals, cite GSA MV-2024-01** to the contracting officer or your reseller.
5. **On state and local deals, find the prepayment statute or policy** in that jurisdiction and reference it.
6. **Align renewals with the fiscal year and funding.** Do not ask the agency to commit future year funds.
7. **Send a proper invoice** so the payment clock starts. I covered [what makes an invoice proper](https://lambdavp.com/blog/prompt-payment-laws-govtech-software-vendors) in an earlier post.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a federal agency pay for a SaaS subscription upfront?

Under GSA guidance, yes when conditions are met. GSA's March 2024 acquisition letter says upfront payment for a SaaS license is not an advance payment when access is granted at the same time as payment, the price is fixed, and the term is fixed and continuous.

### Why are advance payments restricted for federal contracts?

Federal law says a contract payment may not be more than the value of the service already provided or article delivered, unless an advance is authorized by a specific appropriation or other law. GSA notes advance payments to contractors are generally prohibited unless specifically authorized.

### What are GSA's criteria for upfront SaaS payment?

Contemporaneous access with payment; fixed price or fixed price with economic price adjustment; seat, unit, or subscription price for a fixed term; no consumption metric other than quantity; no extra upfront fee for access or discount; and continuous, uninterrupted service for the term.

### Can implementation and training be billed upfront with the subscription?

Not under GSA's MAS cloud guidance. It states that all other cloud computing services and professional services must be paid in arrears under 31 U.S.C. 3324 and 41 U.S.C. 4505. Put services on separate lines from the fixed term license.

### Can an agency prepay a multi-year SaaS deal?

Be careful. GSA's ordering guidance says multi-year cloud commitments must comply with the Bona Fide Needs rule and agencies cannot obligate funds for future years without multi-year contracting authority. Annual upfront billing per year is the safer request.

### Can state and local governments prepay software subscriptions?

Some can, by statute. Minnesota lets counties prepay software development and maintenance services for county data processing equipment, and lets the commissioner allow state agencies to prepay software and IT hosting. Florida lets circuit court clerks prepay county maintenance agreements and subscriptions. Rules vary by state.

### Are there states that prohibit prepayment on software contracts?

Some contracts do. Massachusetts statewide IT contract guides ITS64 and ITS86 say no prepayment should be made for products not yet delivered or services not yet rendered, and require payment within 45 days under the state Bill Payment Policy. Check your contract vehicle.

### What should I do if my government customer still insists on paying in arrears?

Make sure each invoice is proper so payment clocks start, know your prompt payment rights, and plan for the gap with early payment options or financing. See our guides on prompt payment laws and government contract financing.
